10 Ways Businesses Can Use QR Codes

From contactless menus to warranty registration, QR codes have quietly become one of the cheapest bridges between the physical and digital sides of a business.

QM
The QuickMark Team August 8, 2026 9 min read

QR codes are one of the rare pieces of technology that got more useful the more boring they became. Nobody's impressed by a QR code anymore — which is exactly why they now quietly power a huge range of everyday business operations, from the walk-up counter to the back office.

Here are ten uses worth stealing, roughly ordered from the most customer-facing to the most operational.

1. Contactless menus

Print a single QR code on a table tent or sticker instead of laminated menus, and update pricing or specials any time without reprinting anything (provided you're using a dynamic QR code pointed at an editable page). It also sidesteps the hygiene concerns of shared physical menus entirely.

2. Product packaging & traceability

A QR code on packaging can link to sourcing information, care instructions, authenticity verification, or a batch/lot number for recalls — all without cluttering the physical label. Food and cosmetics brands increasingly use this for ingredient transparency; electronics brands use it for warranty registration and firmware downloads.

3. Business cards

A small QR code in the corner of a business card can save your full contact details directly into someone's phone, or link straight to a portfolio, booking page, or LinkedIn profile — turning a card that usually gets set aside into one small action.

4. Event check-in & ticketing

QR codes speed up check-in lines dramatically compared to scanning a name off a printed list — each ticket gets a unique code that staff scan on arrival, and the same approach works for session check-ins, badge printing, and lead capture at trade show booths.

5. Print ads & direct mail

A QR code turns a static print ad or postcard into a trackable, clickable one. Pointed at a dedicated landing page, it also lets you measure how many people actually acted on a specific print placement — something print advertising has historically struggled to do.

6. Payment links

Many payment platforms and invoicing tools support QR codes that open a pre-filled checkout or payment page. This shows up everywhere from market stalls and food trucks to invoices that include a "scan to pay" code alongside the traditional bank details.

7. In-store WiFi access

A WiFi QR code (available in QuickMark's generator) lets a customer join your guest network the instant they scan it — no typing a long, oddly-capitalized password from a sign taped to the wall. Cafés, waiting rooms, and short-term rentals get the most mileage from this one.

8. Employee training & manuals

Placed on equipment, a QR code can link directly to a safety procedure, a maintenance manual, or a short training video — accessible on the spot, on the exact piece of machinery it applies to, instead of buried in a shared drive nobody remembers the folder structure of.

9. Review & feedback collection

A QR code on a receipt, table tent, or checkout counter that links straight to a review page removes almost every step between "I had a good experience" and an actual posted review — which is usually the gap where good intentions quietly die.

10. Loyalty & rewards programs

Digital loyalty cards built around a personal QR code (scanned at checkout to log a visit or purchase) remove the need for a physical punch card that inevitably gets lost, while giving the business cleaner data on repeat customers.
